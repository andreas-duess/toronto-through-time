✈ Aerial photographs

The real city from the air, from 1931

The City of Toronto photographed the city from the air in 1931, 1939, 1954, 1965, 1978 and most years since 1999. Switched on, the survey nearest the year on the slider takes the place of the drawn map, and the 3D buildings and trees step aside, because the photograph has its own. Use it to check the model against what a camera saw. Between surveys the photograph can be a few years off; the note at the top right says which survey it is.

◉ Archive photographs

Pictures taken around the year shown

Camera pins mark City of Toronto Archives photographs taken within two years of the year on the slider. Click a pin to see the pictures there. A pin marks the place named in a picture's title, not where the photographer stood.

◧ Data quality

Colour buildings by where their year comes from

Every building's year has a source: a heritage record, a permit, a named landmark, or an estimate from its neighbourhood. Switched on, each building takes the colour of its source, so you can see what is documented and what is estimated.

Trees are not coloured. They show in today's year only and come from the City of Toronto's 2018 tree canopy study, which mapped the canopy from satellite images and lidar. Where trees stand is from the study; the single trees, about 341,000 of them, and their sizes are drawn to fill that canopy.
